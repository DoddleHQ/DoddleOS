# Comparison-page patterns

Reference for `seo-competitor`. Read when planning a "vs" or "alternatives" page. All
`advisory`; every competitor figure that appears on the page must be `live` or omitted.

## When a comparison page is the right call

- The query intent is *evaluation*: "X vs Y", "X alternatives", "is X better than Y".
- The reader is choosing between named options and wants a decision, not a definition.
- You can say something true and specific about the difference — not just "we are better".

If the intent is informational, a comparison page competes with the wrong format and will not
fit the SERP. Check the dominant result type first.

## Two shapes

| Shape | Targets | Structure |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| `X vs Y` | Head-to-head decision | Intro with verdict, attribute table, where X wins, where Y wins, who should pick which, FAQ |
| `X alternatives` | Buyers leaving a tool | Intro, ranked list with a short reason each, comparison table, how to choose, FAQ |

Keep one page per head-to-head pair that people actually search. Cluster the long tail into a
single "X alternatives" page rather than a page per rival.

## Attribute table rules

- Publish only attributes you can source. Unsourced cells are `⚠️ NOT AVAILABLE`, not guesses.
- Include at least one attribute where the competitor genuinely leads; credibility depends on it.
- Do not copy competitor marketing copy. Describe in your own words.
- Date the comparison and show when it was last updated.

## Trademarks, claims, and schema

- Use a competitor's name descriptively and accurately. Do not imply partnership or endorsement.
- Do not put a competitor's trademark where it reads as your brand in the title or H1.
- Structured data: a comparison page rarely warrants `Product` markup for the *rival*; mark your
  own offering correctly and keep claims matching the page. Validate with `dseo schema`.
- Every claim faces a legal and trust check: if it is not sourced, cut it.

## Provenance on the page

Any competitor metric shown (feature support, pricing, ranking) carries a source and a date, or is
omitted. An unsourced comparison number is the fastest way to lose the reader and the client.
