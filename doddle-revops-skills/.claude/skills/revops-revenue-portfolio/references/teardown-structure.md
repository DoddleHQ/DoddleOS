# Teardown Structure & Portfolio Rules

## The six sections

| Section | Evidence needed |
|---------|-----------------|
| Positioning and narrative | Their own public positioning, dated |
| ICP and segments | Public docs, case studies, or pricing pages, dated |
| Pricing and packaging | A captured pricing page with the date |
| Channels and motion | Public partner, affiliate, or marketplace pages, dated |
| Proof and traction | Cited, dated public claims only |
| Gaps we can exploit | Derived from the evidence above — nothing else |

## Pricing model normalization

`drev portfolio` normalizes pricing models to `seat`, `metered`, `hybrid`, `flat`, `free`, or
`unknown`. This is what makes comparison safe.

## Like-for-like only

Price comparison (min / max / median) is populated **only** when every company in the set
shares one pricing model. A per-seat price beside a usage price is not a benchmark, so the
envelope withholds the comparison and says why.

To compare across models, compare the *shape* — what is metered, what is capped, what is
included — not the headline number.

## Evidence confidence

| Level | Condition |
|-------|-----------|
| `low` | Fewer than three observations, or no usable date |
| `medium` | At least one dated observation within a year |
| `high` | Three or more observations, latest within 180 days |

Report the confidence next to every figure you publish.

## Sourcing rules

- Every observation needs a source URL and an observation date.
- A price without a capture date is `NOT AVAILABLE`.
- A public claim is a claim about their marketing, not proof of their revenue.
- Quote the page's own words where it matters; paraphrase only with the source attached.

## Before publishing

- A teardown is public. Get legal or competitive-intel review on anything about a named
  competitor's pricing or claims.
- Keep the outline as an outline until a human has checked every cell against the source.
